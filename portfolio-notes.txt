NIST Evidence Desk — portfolio notes
====================================

Résumé bullets
--------------
• Built a local FastAPI document Q&A app over 112 pages from two NIST AI risk reports, with page-linked citations, structured table extraction, and a Qwen answer mode backed by source excerpts.
• Benchmarked BM25, BGE-small, and reciprocal rank fusion across three chunking strategies. BM25 with sentence-based chunks reached 57.3% evidence Recall@5 on 32 answerable development questions.
• Evaluated a frozen 20-question set using separate criteria for answer correctness, visible evidence support, and abstention. The app answered 7 of 17 answerable items correctly and correctly declined all 3 unanswerable items.

60-second interview explanation
-------------------------------
I built a local question-answering app for two NIST AI risk reports. The pipeline pins the source PDFs, preserves PDF and printed page numbers, and keeps table cells together. I compared keyword retrieval, semantic retrieval, and a hybrid on labeled development questions. Keyword retrieval with sentence-based chunks was the strongest setting on that draft, with 57.3% evidence Recall@5. The app can then quote a source passage or use a small local model to summarize it. On a frozen 20-question set, it got seven answerable questions right and correctly declined three unanswerable ones. One bad answer cited the right page but misread a grouped list. That failure showed me why answer correctness and citation support need separate evaluation.

Three-minute interview outline
------------------------------
0:00–0:35. State the problem. Users should be able to see exactly where a claim in a document answer came from.
0:35–1:15. Explain ingestion. Preserve the page labels, read two-column sections in order, and extract structured rows. Compare whole rows, fixed windows, and sentence-based chunks.
1:15–1:55. Explain retrieval. BM25 scores keyword matches. BGE scores similarity of passage embeddings. Rank fusion combines rankings. The development results favored BM25 with sentence chunks for evidence Recall@5.
1:55–2:35. Explain evaluation. Freeze the 20 items, score the current browser app once, and distinguish answer correctness, visible evidence support, and abstention. Mention the 10 of 20 overall result alongside the small sample.
2:35–3:00. Show the bias-category failure. A stronger next version would carry full table rows into citations and handle ordered lists directly. Evaluate changes on development questions and reserve a fresh holdout for any new performance claim.

Likely questions
----------------
• Why did you keep BM25? It led the development comparison on the selected metric. Dense retrieval was worth testing for paraphrases, but did not win here.
• What does Recall@5 mean here? For each question, measure the fraction of its labeled evidence excerpts in the top five chunks, then average across questions.
• Why track source support separately? A generated answer can misinterpret a relevant page. The reader also needs enough text on screen to verify the claim, such as the action ID beside its risk labels.
• How did you control leakage? Pilot items stayed in development; known related evidence pages were grouped into one split; the final browser configuration was recorded and run once on the frozen test questions.
• How reliable is the estimate? It is a small, assistant-reviewed evaluation on two reports, with some shared source rows. Three correct abstentions are three examples, rather than evidence of broad reliability.
